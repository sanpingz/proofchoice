Evidence layer · RFQ & procurement sourcing

When an agent picks a supplier, something has to hold the receipt.

ProofChoice sits beside your recommendation agent and commits — cryptographically — to the candidate set it considered, the ranking rule it applied, which candidates paid commission, and which one won. Then it anchors that commitment where the agent cannot go back and edit it.

ProofChoice makes denial expensive. It does not make lying impossible.

    

      That boundary is the product, not a caveat bolted onto it. A Merkle proof shows inclusion and
      can never show that a set was complete; coverage shows what was asked, never what exists; a
      signature binds a commitment to a key, not to a truth. Everything below is built to stay inside
      that line — and to say so out loud when it reaches it.
    

  







  

    

      
The problem

      
An agent recommended one supplier out of eight. Who checks?

      

        Procurement is moving inside models. The buyer sees one answer and a paragraph of reasoning;
        the seven suppliers who were not recommended see nothing at all. Every party in that
        transaction has a question, and today nobody can answer any of them after the fact.
      

    

    

      

        
The buyer asks

        
“Was this ranked on merit, or on commission?”

        
The agent's own explanation is generated by the same system under question.
          A screenshot of it is not evidence — it can be regenerated, edited, or quietly reworded
          after the fact.

      

      

        
The supplier asks

        
“Was I even in the running?”

        
A supplier that was silently pruned from the candidate set looks identical
          to one that was considered and lost. There is no artefact that separates the two, so
          there is nothing to dispute.

      

      

        
The platform asks

        
“How do I answer that without opening my ranking model?”

        
The ranking logic is the business. Any answer that requires publishing it,
          or handing an auditor a live seat inside the pipeline, will not be given.

      

      

        
The regulator asks

        
“Show me the decision as it stood that day.”

        
Logs are written by the party under audit and mutable by the same party.
          A record that its author can rewrite is a record of nothing in particular.

      

    

  







  

    

      
How it works

      
Six stages. Each one's output is the next one's only input.

      

        Stage 1 is yours and stays untouched — ProofChoice never sees your ranking logic, never
        re-orders, never blocks and never delays. It starts the moment you have an answer.
      

    

    

      

        
STAGE 01

        
Recommend

        
Your agent calls its sources, filters, ranks and picks a winner. The plugin
          does not participate — it is not in the path.

        
plugin: inactive

      

      

        
STAGE 02

        
Snapshot

        
Salt and hash each candidate, sort by id, build a Merkle tree, assemble the
          snapshot and hash it. Salting matters: name plus price is low-entropy and an unsalted leaf
          is brute-forceable from a shared path.

        
merkle_root · snapshot_hash

      

      

        
STAGE 03

        
Sign

        
ECDSA P-256 over the snapshot hash, with the key bound to a named operator in
          an on-chain key registry — otherwise “key compromise” repudiates every past signature.

        
signature · signer_key_id

      

      

        
STAGE 04

        
Anchor

        
Write-once, reverting on a repeated hash. The commitment now exists somewhere
          the agent's operator cannot quietly amend it. The preimage stays off-chain with a named
          custodian and a retention SLA.

        
anchor(snapshot_hash, sig, key_id)

      

      

        
STAGE 05

        
Receipts

        
Requests go to every supplier in the category registry — not to the list
          the platform declared — and signed receipts come back through a neutral relayer, never
          through the party under audit.

        
the load-bearing stage

      

      

        
STAGE 06

        
Audit

        
Pull, recompute, compare. The verifier is pure — no network, no filesystem,
          no clock — so a third party runs it with no access to your infrastructure and trusts
          nothing you assert.

        
PASS · PARTIAL · FAIL · INCONCLUSIVE

      

    

    

      Two of those decisions carry most of the weight. Draw receipt requests from the platform's own
      declared set and a pruned supplier is never asked, produces no denial, and the audit is
      circular. Route receipts through the platform and the accused party is carrying the evidence
      that indicts it. Both failure modes are switchable in the demo, so
      you can watch detection collapse rather than take our word for it.
    

  







  

    

      
The bounded claim

      
What it proves, and what it will never prove

      

        Most trust products publish only the left-hand column. The right-hand one is why anybody
        should believe the left — and both are returned as fields by GET /health, so no
        interface has to paraphrase them.
      

    

    

      

        
What it proves

        
Recomputable by anyone, from the evidence blob, with no access to us.

        

          
✓

          
The commitment predates the dispute

            
The anchor is write-once and timestamped before any complaint existed.
              A story assembled afterwards no longer fits the hash.

        

        

          
✓

          
The evidence has not been edited since

            
Every served copy is re-hashed against the anchor. Two bytes changed in a
              490-byte snapshot flips roughly half the output bits — and the demo shows you exactly that.

        

        

          
✓

          
A named operator signed it

            
The signing key resolves from the on-chain registry, not from whatever the
              platform hands the auditor, with rotation history attached.

        

        

          
✓

          
The winner was inside the committed set

            
Merkle inclusion against a tree rebuilt from the evidence — never against
              a root the platform simply asserts.

        

        

          
✓

          
Which suppliers were asked, and what they said

            
Signed receipts from the independent registry, recorded as three distinct
              states: affirm, deny, uncovered.

        

      


      

        
What it cannot

        
Stated as plainly as the column beside it, because an overclaim here is a bug.

        

          
×

          
It cannot prove a set was complete

            
A Merkle proof shows inclusion. No root, ever, shows that nothing was left
              out. Only registry coverage reaches that question — and only probabilistically.

        

        

          
×

          
It cannot prove the ranking was fair

            
It commits to the rule you declared, not to the rule you ran. What it
              removes is the ability to declare a different one afterwards.

        

        

          
×

          
“Nobody objected” is not “nobody was asked”

            
Uncovered suppliers are counted separately and never folded into
              agreement. If an interface ever makes that number easy to overlook, the mechanism has
              been defeated by its own UI.

        

        

          
×

          
A signature binds to a key, not to a truth

            
The platform is signing its own account of its own decision. What the key
              registry adds is that it cannot later disown the account.

        

        

          
×

          
Custody makes withholding visible, not impossible

            
The chain guarantees the anchor persists, never the preimage. A withheld
              blob returns a custody failure with a name attached — attributable, not prevented.

        

      

    

  







  

    

      
Detection math

      
The honest numbers on catching a silent prune

      

        Asking every supplier on every proof does not scale, so coverage is sampled and detection is
        hypergeometric. A platform prunes k of n registry members; an auditor samples
        s of them.
      

    

    

      

        
P(detect) = 1 − C(n−k, s) / C(n, s)

        

          
nk pruneds sampledP(detect)

Against a single-supplier prune in a small registry, only
          near-total coverage gives high per-proof detection.

        

          That is the uncomfortable half, and we would rather you read it here than discover it in
          a pilot. The recovery is that detection compounds across proofs — over m independent
          samples it is 1 − (1−p)m, so a per-proof 0.5 reaches 96.9% by the fifth
          proof.
        

        

          Which is an argument for auditing a corpus, not for claiming more about any single
          proof than the numbers support. The live endpoint GET /detection returns these
          figures for your own n, k and s.

Questions

      
The ones worth asking first

    

    

      Does this see or slow down my ranking model?
      
No, on both counts. Stage 1 belongs entirely to your agent; ProofChoice starts once you
        already have a winner. It never re-ranks, blocks, delays or reorders, and the ranking rule is
        committed as an identifier — rr.value-weighted.v2 — not as its
        implementation.

    

    

      What stops me committing to a candidate set I made up?
      
Nothing at the commit stage — and that is stated deliberately rather than papered over.
        pc_attest commits to whatever set you declare, and stages 2 to 4 will attest
        perfectly to a pruned pool. Only stage 5 reaches it: receipts are requested from every member
        of the independent category registry, so a supplier you left out is still asked, and signs a
        denial that lands on the record. That asymmetry is the mechanism, not a gap in it.

    

    

      Is this a blockchain?
      
No, and we will not let it be described as one. The reference implementation's ledger is a
        single-operator append-only log: it does not resist its own operator deleting it. The
        cryptography is real and independently recomputable; the decentralisation is not there yet.
        Saying so is the only version of this product worth selling.

    

    

      What happens if the evidence blob disappears?
      
The anchor still verifies and the audit returns a custody failure with a named
        holder attached — never a chain failure, and never a silent pass. Custody has five states,
        including expired retention, which returns INCONCLUSIVE rather than being
        dressed up as a partial pass. Withholding becomes visible and attributable. It does not
        become impossible.

    

    

      Can I verify a proof without trusting you?
      
That is the requirement the whole design is built around. The verification algorithm is
        pure — no network, no filesystem, no clock — so you can run it against an evidence blob with
        no access to our infrastructure. The console goes further and recomputes every hash in your
        browser rather than displaying the server's verdict, so if the two ever disagreed, the
        disagreement itself would be the finding.

    

    

      Why procurement rather than consumer search?
      
Because the enhanced layer needs a bounded supplier registry to draw receipt requests from,
        and consumer search does not have one. Without that registry the audit is circular: you only
        ever ask the suppliers the platform already admitted to considering. Scoping to RFQ and
        procurement sourcing is honest about where the mechanism actually works.

      push the clock past retention, edit the platform's stored copy byte by byte. Then re-run the
      audit and see which check catches it — and which one does not.
